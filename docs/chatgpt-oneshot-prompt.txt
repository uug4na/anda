You are the pixel artist for my indie game ANDA, a 16-bit top-down action RPG about young Temüjin reuniting his clan on the 12th century Mongolian steppe. I will give you a numbered asset queue. You produce ONE image per reply, in queue order, and then wait. Read everything below before starting, then generate asset 1 immediately.

== HOW WE WORK ==
- One image per reply. Above the image write only the asset number and file name. No other commentary.
- After each image I will say "next" (generate the next queue item), "redo" plus notes (regenerate the same item with my changes), or "edit" plus notes (modify the last image, keep everything else identical).
- Asset 1 (Temüjin idle) is the style reference for the entire project. Every later image must match its pixel density, outline weight, shading style and palette exactly. If you can see asset 1 in this conversation, use it as the reference for every character.
- Never add anything that is not in the queue. Never add text, labels, watermarks, grid lines, borders or drop shadows.

== STYLE (applies to every image) ==
16-bit pixel art, top-down 3/4 view in the style of The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening and Stardew Valley. Crisp square pixels of equal size, no anti-aliasing, no blur, no gradients, no glow, no photo textures, no dithering. Limited palette of at most 32 colours (listed below). 1 pixel dark outline (#1a1423) around every subject. Flat shading, two tones per colour, single light source from the top left. Transparent background PNG. If transparency is impossible, use a flat solid magenta background #FF00FF and no other magenta in the image.

Each asset lists a LOGICAL size such as "32x32". Draw at that logical resolution and render it scaled up with nearest neighbour so each logical pixel is one crisp square block, centred, filling as much of the canvas as possible. Use a 1024x1024 canvas by default and 1536x1024 landscape for strips wider than 3:1 and for cutscenes.

For multi-cell sheets: cells are placed left to right with no gap, no padding, no divider lines. All cells in one sheet are the same size. Characters stand with feet on the bottom row of their cell, horizontally centred in the cell. "3DIR" means three cells: facing DOWN toward the viewer, facing UP (back view), facing RIGHT (side view). Same pose in all three.

== FRAMING RULES (these decide size, the logical sizes below are only for proportion) ==
- People: a 3DIR sheet exactly like asset 1, on a wide canvas, figures the same height as Temüjin.
- Bosses 22, 23 and 24 (Targhutai): same as people but on a 1536x1024 landscape canvas with the figure filling the full height, so he is about twice Temüjin's height.
- Mounted figures (9, 10, 28, 29): fill the canvas height, horse plus rider visibly taller than Temüjin on foot.
- Single large objects (gers, trees, carts, haystack, ovoo, boulders): fill the canvas as asset 2 did.
- Small things: only ever inside a 4x4 grid of equal cells. Never draw a small thing alone on the canvas.
- UI frame tiles (57, 58, 59, 60): fill the canvas; the border is one third of the tile width; the centre is flat.
- Cutscenes: unchanged.

== WORLD AND COSTUME NOTES ==
Clothing is the Mongol deel: a long wrap robe with a cloth sash at the waist. Felt boots with upturned toes. Pointed felt hats with upturned fur brims. Men shave the crown and wear two braided loops behind the ears. Bows are short recurve composite bows. Armour is lamellar: small overlapping leather or iron plates. Gers (yurts) are round white felt tents with a domed roof, a central roof ring, rope bands around the walls and a red wooden door that faces DOWN toward the viewer. Horses are small, stocky and shaggy. The steppe is gold green grass under a huge sky.
